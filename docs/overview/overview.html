Product overview

AI Recruiter OS

Shortlist hundreds of resumes in seconds, with a reason for every decision.

For campus placement cells and small recruitment teams that still shortlist by hand.

The problem

Every opening brings a pile of resumes that someone reads one by one. It takes hours, two people rarely shortlist the same way, and good candidates get missed late in the pile.

How it works

1

Paste the job

Optionally add must-have skills and a minimum CGPA.

2

Add resumes

Up to 200 at once: PDF, Word (DOCX) or text.

3

Get the shortlist

Seconds later, everyone is ranked and explained.

What you get for every candidate

A suggestion: Shortlist Maybe Not a fit
A score from 0 to 100, to compare at a glance
A reason: "Has 2 of 3 must-haves; missing Python."
Warnings, such as "No CGPA found on the resume"
Interview questions that probe the gaps
Exports: filters, CSV download, copy shortlisted emails

Private by design. Resumes are read for one screening, then discarded. Nothing is stored. Files it can't read, such as scanned images, are listed for a person to check instead of being quietly ranked last.

How the score works

PartWeightWhat it measures
Must-haves60%Share of the recruiter's must-haves on the resume
Other skills30%Share of the job description's other skills found
Wording10%How closely the wording matches (cosine similarity)

If a part doesn't apply, for example no must-haves were given, it's left out and the others are scaled up to fill 100.

How suggestions are decided

SituationSuggestion
CGPA found and below the cut-offNot a fit
Has every must-haveShortlist
Missing one of two or more must-havesMaybe
Missing more than thatNot a fit
No must-haves givenScore: 60+ Shortlist, 35–59 Maybe

Simple rules on purpose: a recruiter can predict every decision. Someone ruled out only by CGPA is called out, in case the cut-off is relaxed.

Accuracy details that matter

  • "Java" never matches "JavaScript". Skills match only as whole terms, and "C++", "C#" and "Node.js" match exactly.
  • "Eager to excel" isn't Microsoft Excel. Words that are also everyday English only count when capitalised as a skill.
  • Spellings are understood. "Postgres" counts as PostgreSQL, and "ReactJS" as React.
  • CGPA is read carefully. "CGPA 8.20 / 10", "8.5 CGPA" and "C.G.P.A - 7.9" all work. A 4-point GPA or a percentage is ignored, never guessed.
  • Missing information is flagged, not punished. No CGPA on a resume means a warning, not a rejection.
  • Unreadable files go to a person. Scanned images, old .doc files and damaged files are listed separately.

Built with

  • Backend: Python 3.11, FastAPI, pypdf
  • Frontend: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, safe against script and spreadsheet-formula injection
  • Deployment: a Dockerfile for cloud hosts

Honest limits and what's next

  • It finds whether a skill appears, not how good someone is at it. The interview questions help check.
  • Scanned resumes need a person, because reading text from images (OCR) isn't built yet.
  • Next: pilot with a placement cell, deploy, then optional AI summaries with the organisation's consent.
